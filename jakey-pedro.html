Jakey Pedro

Biography

Jakey Pedro is an Australian contemporary artist whose bold, instinctive paintings blur the boundaries between neo-expressionism, pop culture and personal mythology. Growing up in the coastal landscape of Noosa Heads, Queensland, his work draws on childhood memories, surfing culture and the untamed spirit of the American rodeo—using it as a powerful metaphor for the unpredictability of life and humanity's constant attempt to maintain control.

Working from his studio on Sydney's Northern Beaches, Jakey paints with an immediacy that embraces spontaneity and risk. Layers of acrylic, enamel, charcoal and oil stick combine with washed-out fluorescent pigments, expressive mark-making and handwritten text to create works that are both playful and emotionally charged. Each painting balances raw energy with careful intuition, inviting viewers to uncover their own narratives within the surface.

Influenced by artists including Julian Schnabel, Ben Quilty and Jean-Michel Basquiat, Jakey has developed a distinctive visual language that is unmistakably his own. His paintings celebrate imperfection, humour and freedom, where oversized florals, cowboys, perfume bottles and recurring symbols become contemporary icons infused with optimism, nostalgia and wit.

Transitioning from a blue-collar career in plastering to full-time artistic practice, Jakey has rapidly established himself as one of Australia's most exciting emerging contemporary painters. His vibrant, highly collectible works are held in private collections across the country and continue to resonate with collectors drawn to their authenticity, vitality and unmistakable sense of joy.

Artist Statement

“I paint instinctively. I don't overthink it or begin with a fixed outcome. I let the work evolve through energy, colour and whatever feels honest in the moment. Painting is where I can make sense of the chaos, humour and contradictions of everyday life. ”

My work draws on everything that has shaped me, from growing up in Noosa and the freedom of surfing, to old Western films, music, fashion, advertising and the endless stream of imagery that surrounds us. Cowboys appear throughout my paintings as recurring characters. For me, they're less about the American West and more about the idea of navigating life, holding on while everything around you feels unpredictable. They're symbols of resilience, vulnerability and the balancing act we all perform.

I'm interested in creating paintings that feel raw, playful and immediate. I work quickly, building layers of acrylic, enamel, charcoal and oil stick with loose gestures, handwritten text and bold fluorescent colour. Fragments of language, borrowed from films, songs, cigarette packets or passing conversations, find their way into the work, where they lose their original meaning and become open to interpretation.

Influenced by artists such as Jean-Michel Basquiat, Julian Schnabel and Ben Quilty, my practice embraces imperfection and spontaneity. I want each painting to retain the energy of its making, where every mark, drip and erasure becomes part of the final image.

Ultimately, I want the work to feel alive. I hope it invites curiosity, sparks conversation and reminds us not to take life, or art, too seriously. There is beauty in contradiction, freedom in not having all the answers, and joy in simply letting the paint lead the way.
